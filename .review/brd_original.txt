1. Executive summary
LARA is a standalone DCP product: a complete, AI-native Computerized Accounting System (CAS) built for Philippine businesses. It keeps the books, issues BIR-compliant invoices, prepares tax files, and sends electronic invoices to the BIR on its own, without needing any other product. It is built from the ground up as a DCP product, so every business that adopts it runs on the same codebase through configuration rather than a custom build.
The system must do three things well:
Pass BIR registration and later examination. The design follows the BIR's Annex B checklist, which every registered system must strictly comply with.
Produce valid electronic invoices. An invoice produced by a CAS only counts as an electronic invoice if the system can transmit the required data to the BIR. LARA includes this capability as a built-in module.
Run a real finance operation. Double-entry ledger, receivables, payables, bank, inventory, fixed assets, tax, and reports, with AI that prepares work for people to approve.
Stand up to an audit. Auditors, internal audit, and examiners get the evidence they ask for from the system itself, not from spreadsheets prepared by hand.
Timing is driven by the e-invoicing mandate. Covered taxpayers must move to electronic invoicing by December 31, 2026, and later groups follow under separate BIR issuances. Phase 1 is therefore limited to what registration, the Permit to Issue, and electronic invoicing need, so the product is ready for the businesses that must comply first.
Vision
One product that fits any industry and any size of business. A sole proprietor, a mid-sized trading company, and a universal bank should all run the same release, differing only in configuration: chart of accounts, tax types, document series, approval rules, and the industry pack they switch on. Nothing in the design is written for one client, and no client gets a fork of the codebase.
Where we start
The first industry pack is for banks and other financial institutions, because that is where DCP is having its current conversations, and because banks sit inside the first e-invoicing group as Large Taxpayers Service registrants. Banks are also the hardest case: many branches, gross receipts tax instead of VAT, separate books for the regular banking unit and the foreign currency deposit unit, heavy documentary stamp tax, feeds from core banking, and examiners from the BIR, the BSP, and internal audit all asking for evidence. A system that satisfies a bank will satisfy a retailer with room to spare. The requirements in section 9.11 are therefore a pack that is switched on, not a change to the core, and the product is not limited to banks.
2. Philippine business practices
Local finance teams follow many habits that general accounting software does not handle out of the box. Covering them well removes the spreadsheets and paper logs that clients otherwise keep on the side. The full requirements are in section 9.10. In summary, they fall into six groups:
Paper trail and approvals: check and disbursement vouchers with signature blocks, check signatory rules, counter receipts, check release days, and numbered supplementary receipts.
Cash handling: post-dated checks, daily collection reports with cash count, deposit slips, revolving funds, and cash advance liquidation.
Tax paperwork around each transaction: supplier and customer certificates (2303, sworn declarations, zero-rating certificates), 2307 follow-up, withholding timing under EOPT, tax payment records, and the BIR filing calendar.
Organization: head office and branch accounts, per-branch books, and local government obligations such as business permits and real property tax.
Local channels: local bank statement formats, InstaPay, PESONet, and QR Ph references, e-wallets, and online marketplace payouts.
Industry habits: progress billing with retention, lease deposits and advance rent, statutory discounts, and employee loans.
Rules to confirm with the tax advisor
A few items depend on specific regulations that should be checked before build: output VAT relief on uncollected receivables under EOPT, withholding on online marketplace and e-wallet payouts, the scope of statutory discounts beyond senior citizens and PWDs, and current rules on binding and submitting loose-leaf books. Their requirements are written so the rates and rules are settings, not code.
3. Business background
Philippine businesses are moving to electronic invoicing and electronic sales reporting, which the BIR receives through its Electronic Invoicing System (EIS). Many of them still keep manual books or use general software that was not built for BIR rules. The first e-invoicing group includes e-commerce sellers (except micro), Large Taxpayers Service registrants, large taxpayers under the EOPT Act, and businesses using a CAS or CBA with electronic invoicing, so the immediate market is sizeable and time-bound.
Problem. Without a registered CAS that produces compliant invoices and books and can send e-invoices, a business cannot meet the new rules, and faces penalties and disallowed deductions.
Opportunity. LARA gives businesses one compliant system for books, invoices, tax files, and e-invoicing, with AI built around Philippine rules. For DCP, it is a new recurring product rather than a one-off build.

Phase 1 scope discipline
Phases are ordered by dependency, not by calendar date. Phase 1 holds only what a business needs to register the system, obtain the Permit to Issue, keep compliant books, and issue and transmit electronic invoices. Anything that does not serve those four goals belongs in a later phase, however useful it looks. Dates are set per release once the build team and scope are fixed.

7. Regulatory basis
The system must comply with the issuances below. They change often, so the tax advisor confirms the current list before registration.
RR 9-2009: electronic records, mandatory fields in books, and audit trail. Records must show a trail from the source document to the summarized financial accounts.
RMC 5-2021 and RMO 9-2021: simplified registration and the Annex B Standard Functional and Technical Requirements.
RMC 10-2020: suspended the Permit to Use and replaced it with an Acknowledgement Certificate carrying a control number that must appear on the invoices the system produces.
RR 7-2024 as amended by RR 11-2024, and RMC 77-2024 (EOPT): invoice content and the shift from official receipts to invoices.
RR 11-2025 as amended by RR 26-2025: electronic invoicing and sales reporting.
RR 16-2006: the Standard Audit File format for soft copies of books.
RR 17-2013 as amended by RR 5-2014: record retention (10 years).
RA 10173, the Data Privacy Act: protection of personal data such as names, addresses, and TINs.
Draft circular on electronic invoices (consulted August 25, 2026): the Permit to Issue, the QR code, what counts as an electronic invoice, correction rules, and coverage. Still a draft, so every detail is held as a setting.
Pending rule to watch
The draft circular has since been discussed with industry and circulated. It confirms that covered taxpayers may use in-house systems, bought software, or a local solution provider, and it adds the Permit to Issue, the QR code, and the correction rules now written into sections 10 and 11. Commentators have asked for more time, and a foreign solution provider may not be allowed to serve Philippine taxpayers. Nothing is final until the circular is published, so section 11 keeps all of it as configuration.
8. Core business processes
Order to cash
Quotation, then sales order, then delivery (if goods), then invoice posted with a BIR serial number, then sent to the BIR EIS by the e-invoicing module, then collection recorded, then withholding certificate received (2307) and logged, then bank reconciled.
Procure to pay
Purchase request, then purchase order, then goods receipt, then supplier invoice captured (AI) and matched, then withholding computed, then bill approved and posted, then payment and 2307 issued to supplier, then bank reconciled.
Record to report
Daily postings, then bank reconciliation, then accruals and depreciation, then close checklist, then period lock, then financial statements, then BIR books and tax files, then filing and archive.
Correct a mistake
Electronic invoice: never deleted or changed. To lower the amount, issue a credit note that refers to the original. To raise it, issue a new invoice that refers to the original. Non-electronic invoices keep the older route of void with a reason and approval, then reissue. Journal entry: reversing entry with a reference to the original. Nothing posted is ever edited or deleted.
Close a period
Checklist tasks assigned and worked through, then accounts substantiated with their schedules, then review and sign-off, then the period locked. Anything found later comes in as an approved audit adjustment that is shown separately, never as a change to what was already reported.

9.0 Shared building blocks
The modules that follow are built from a small number of shared engines. Anything that looks like a new register, calendar, queue, or import is configuration of one of these, not a new piece of software. This keeps the product small enough to test, and it is why a new industry pack is settings rather than a build.
ID | Shared building block | Priority | Phase
FR-SH-001 | Posting engine. The only way anything reaches the ledger. Balance check, period control, dimensions, control accounts, audit trail, and reversal rules live here once. | M | 1
FR-SH-002 | Document engine. One registry of document types (invoice, credit note, voucher, collection receipt, delivery receipt and so on), each with its own series, layout, rules, and whether it is an electronic invoice. New document types are configuration. | M | 1
FR-SH-003 | Party master. Customers, suppliers, employees, and banks are one record with roles, so tax status, certificates, limits, and balances are held once. | M | 1
FR-SH-004 | Tax engine. VAT, percentage taxes, withholding, and stamp taxes as rules with rates, effective dates, and evidence of the version used. Every module asks the engine; no module computes tax itself. | M | 1
FR-SH-005 | Approval and limits engine. Approval routes, amount limits, signatory rules, credit limits, and segregation-of-duties checks in one place, used by every document type. | M | 1
FR-SH-006 | Ingestion pipeline. One path for everything arriving from outside: spreadsheets, bank files, POS closings, core banking journals, payroll journals, supplier e-invoices, and migrations. Connect, stage, validate, map, then propose drafts; failures land in the work queue. | M | 1
FR-SH-007 | Reconciliation framework. One way to match two sets of records and explain the difference: bank against ledger, payments against invoices, sub-ledgers against control accounts, books against returns, and documents against what the BIR accepted. | M | 1

FR-SH-011 | Reporting layer. Statutory books and BIR outputs are locked templates over the ledger; everything else is built in the same report builder, with one place for headers, print templates, and exports. | M | 1

11. E-invoicing module
LARA sends electronic invoices and sales data to the BIR itself, so no separate middleware is needed. Where DCP’s SyncTax middleware already does part of this work, LARA reuses it rather than building a second transmission layer. Two duties are separate under the draft rules: issuing electronic invoices, which covered taxpayers must do by December 31, 2026, and reporting sales data, which follows its own guidelines and a BIR notice. LARA must be able to do both, with transmission switched on per company. Each client also needs a Permit to Issue before it can issue electronic invoices, separate from the Acknowledgement Certificate. For clients who prefer an outside provider, LARA can hand data over through its API, noting that the draft rules appear to allow local providers only.

12. AI requirements and differentiators
Guiding rule: AI prepares, a person posts.
The BIR does not allow posted records to be edited, and a registered system must show who did what. So every AI feature in this document prepares work, and a named person reviews and posts it.
12.1 Guardrails (apply to every AI feature)
ID | Requirement
AI-GR-001 | AI can only create drafts, suggestions, and alerts. It never posts, voids, approves, or changes a posted record.
AI-GR-002 | Every suggestion is stored with model version, input reference, and confidence; the audit trail records the person who accepted or rejected it.
AI-GR-003 | The AI layer is separate from the registered accounting rules, so model updates do not change how invoices or books are produced.
AI-GR-004 | AI respects the user's permissions; it cannot show data the user cannot see.
AI-GR-005 | Client data is not used to train shared models without written consent; personal data handled per the Data Privacy Act.
AI-GR-006 | Each AI feature can be switched off per company.
AI-GR-007 | Answers about numbers always show the source records they came from.

15. Data migration and go-live
Cut-over date. Agree it with the tax advisor, ideally at the start of a month or quarter, and after the Acknowledgement Certificate is issued.
Migrate these records: 
Chart of accounts
Customers, suppliers, and items
Opening trial balance
Open receivables and payables
Fixed asset register
Inventory on hand
Close the old series. Record the last number used in the old books or invoices, and state which books cover which months.
Agree how much history to load. Examiners can look back several years, so decide which prior periods are migrated in full, which are summarised, and which stay in the old system with a documented handover.
Test before going live. Use a sandbox to test e-invoice transmission, run a parallel month where possible, and get sign-off on reconciled opening balances.
Train by role. Provide short videos and a printed quick guide for billing staff covering voids, reprints, and downtime invoices.
16. Architecture and technology
Decision: DCP builds and owns the system, written from the ground up with Claude Code. No third-party accounting product is embedded, extended, or copied, so no outside licence terms apply to LARA.
The build stack is still to be chosen. Two options fit:
Option | What it means | Pros | Cons
A. Open-source Python framework | Use a permissively licensed, metadata-driven Python framework as the base and write every accounting module ourselves. | Metadata-driven forms and workflows come for free, which suits the localisation approach in the architecture principles. | MariaDB by default, and a smaller hiring pool than JavaScript stacks.
B. DCP’s own stack | TypeScript (Next.js and NestJS, as in Daedalus) with PostgreSQL. | Full control; team familiarity; PostgreSQL row-level security and strong locking for serial numbers; shared tooling with other DCP products. | We design our own metadata layer.

Guidance
Because the code is written with Claude Code, the volume of code to be written matters less than it used to, and the choice should be made on what the team can review, test, and support with confidence. The posting engine, the tax rules, and the audit trail are the parts that deserve the most human review.
Architecture principles
A small, well-tested posting engine. It is the only way to write to the ledger. Every module posts through it.
Metadata-driven records. Forms, fields, and workflows come from metadata, so Philippine localization lives in its own package (the "regional" pattern).
Event-driven links. E-invoicing, AI, notifications, and integrations work from events.
AI as its own service. The AI service reads data through the same permission checks and writes only drafts.
Multi-tenant from day one. Clients are configured, not forked.
Size and industry neutral. The same posting engine serves a small business and a bank. Differences are settings, industry packs, and infrastructure, never a separate build or a client-specific branch of the code.
Build engines, not features. Before anything is built, the question is which shared building block in 9.0 it belongs to. A requirement that fits none of them is either genuinely new or, more often, a feature that should have been configuration.
17. Streamlining and reuse
The fastest way to deliver is to build less. DCP already owns most of the hard parts, and each reuse below also lowers the cost of onboarding every business after the first, which is objective OBJ-6.
Reuse SyncTax for transmission. The BIR e-invoicing middleware already exists. LARA should call it rather than write a second transmission layer, and SyncTax keeps the permits, signing keys, and retry logic.
Reuse JANUS for attachments and retention. Ten years of documents, versions, and search are a solved problem in JANUS (FR-PL-001).
Reuse GAIA as the AI gateway. Model access, permissions, logging, and per-company switches come from GAIA, so the AI guardrails in 12.1 are enforced in one place.
Build with Claude Code and Daedalus. The product is written from the ground up with Claude Code, with DCP engineers owning design, review, and testing. This is what makes a build of this size practical, and it keeps the code, the tests, and the documentation in one place.
One BIR rules service. Rates, ATC codes, thresholds, form layouts, and deadlines maintained once by a small team and shared by LARA, SyncTax, and LISA, so a rule change is made in one place.
One go-live cockpit. Registration pack, permit pack, migration, opening balances, and the parallel run in a single guided flow, run by the client with DCP checking, instead of a custom project each time.
One approval inbox. The agent inbox (AI-029) becomes the single queue for bills, collections, close, and tax work, rather than a separate queue in every module.
One treasury workflow. Vouchers, signatory rules, check release days, and the post-dated check calendar in one flow, not three screens that staff reconcile by hand.
Industry configuration packs. Banking and financial institutions first, then retail, services, construction, leasing, and non-profit, each shipped as versioned settings so a new client is configured, not built.
Self-serve trial with sample data. A sandbox company with realistic Philippine transactions lets buyers and examiners see the system without a demo call, and doubles as the test data for registration.
What the review simplified
A panel of accounting and process reviewers went through every module. Their conclusion was that the requirement list was long because the same idea appeared many times under different names. The decisions below cut the number of things to build without cutting what the product does.
Decision | What it replaces | Effect
One document engine (FR-SH-002) | Separate handling for invoices, credit and debit notes, vouchers, collection and acknowledgement receipts, delivery receipts, billing statements, and cash versus charge invoices. | Roughly a dozen document requirements become configuration of one type registry, and a new document type takes a setting instead of a release.
One ingestion pipeline (FR-SH-006) | Spreadsheet import, guided migration, bank statement import, POS sales and cashier closing import, payroll journal import, core banking interface, and the supplier e-invoice inbox. | Seven import paths become one pipeline with adapters, one validation step, and one place where failures are fixed.
One reconciliation framework (FR-SH-007) | Bank reconciliation, payment application, account substantiation, sub-ledger against control account, stock against ledger, books against returns, and books against what the BIR accepted. | One matching and explanation model, reused seven ways, and the same evidence trail for auditors every time.
One obligations calendar (FR-SH-009) | Filing tracker, BIR calendar, certificate and permit expiries, loose-leaf binding tracker, local government obligations, correspondence deadlines, and bank guarantee expiries. | Seven registers become obligation types with owners and reminders.
One work queue (FR-SH-010) | Approval queues per module, AI agent inbox, e-invoice rejection queue, import failure queue, unmatched bank lines, and control exceptions. | Staff learn one screen, and nothing waits in a queue nobody opens.
One schedule engine (FR-SH-008) | Depreciation, deferred revenue and expense, recurring invoices and journals, lease schedules, and amortisations. | Five scheduled-entry features become one engine with different schedule types.
One party master (FR-SH-003) | Separate customer, supplier, employee, and bank records, each with its own tax status, certificates, and limits. | A supplier who is also a customer is one record, so withholding, compliance files, and balances stop disagreeing.
Six AI agents instead of a feature list | Forty-plus separately specified AI features. | The same behaviours ship as capture, coding and matching, compliance watch, cash and collections, close, and audit support, sharing one inbox and one guardrail set.
Dropped or folded in | Shareholder register (removed), dunning as a module (folded into collections), bank guarantee register (folded into obligations). | Less to build, and nothing a finance team actually needs is lost.
Phase 1 AI trimmed to four | Seven AI differentiators previously planned for Phase 1. | Registration and e-invoicing stay the focus; the readers and digitizers move to Phase 2 where they belong.
18. Assumptions and constraints
Each implementing business names a tax advisor to review templates and filings.
Each implementing business applies for its own BIR permits and EIS credentials.
BIR technical specifications may change during the build, so they are held as configuration rather than code.
The system is delivered as cloud software by default; on-premise is optional and priced separately.
Full payroll, manufacturing, and CRM stay out of scope.
The December 31, 2026 date for the first covered group holds unless the Commissioner extends it again, which RR 26-2025 allows.
19. Risks
Risk | Impact | Response
Phase 1 grows beyond what registration and e-invoicing need | High | Hold the Phase 1 line described in section 5; review every addition against the four Phase 1 goals; run the e-invoicing module as a parallel workstream.
BIR rules change during the build | High | Keep tax rules, templates, and thresholds in configuration; track new issuances weekly.
Examiner finds a gap after registration | High | Internal Annex B test suite run on every release; advisor review before filing.
DCP liability as joint signatory | Medium | Contract terms on responsibilities, support levels, and re-registration duties.
AI makes a wrong suggestion that gets approved | Medium | Confidence thresholds, clear "suggested" labels, sampling reviews, and the pre-filing checks.
Poor source data during migration | Medium | Migration helper with validation; reconciled opening balances before go-live.
The e-invoicing circular is still a draft | High | Keep permits, QR content, delivery, and correction rules as settings; watch for the final text; treat any change as configuration rather than code.
Only local providers may act as e-invoicing solution providers | Medium | Confirm which Philippine DCP entity contracts, hosts, signs the Joint Sworn Statement, and acts as provider before the first client signs.
Clients expect filing and payment inside the product | Medium | Partner with an accredited electronic tax service provider for Phase 2 rather than building filing and payment ourselves (FR-PH-030).
The name LARA is already used elsewhere | Low | Check trademark records and domains before registration, and register a lockup such as "LARA by Datum". The declared software name is printed on every report, so changing it later needs an amended permit.
Bank requirements pull the product toward custom work | High | Everything bank-specific ships as a configuration pack on the same core. Any request that cannot be expressed as settings, a pack, or a general feature is refused or generalised before it is built.
Financial institution security and procurement reviews take longer than other sectors | Medium | Prepare the security, hosting, and outsourcing pack early (NFR-SEC-013) and treat it as a product deliverable rather than a per-deal effort.

20. Open questions
Coverage. Which taxpayer groups do we target first, and do we support voluntary adopters who are not yet covered?
Institution types. Which institutions do we design the first pack around: universal and commercial banks, thrift and rural banks, or financing and lending companies?
Inventory. Do we bring inventory into Phase 1 by default, since businesses selling goods need the Inventory Book at registration?
Close and substantiation. Do close management and account substantiation ship in Phase 1 for everyone, or only for larger clients?
Scale. What size of business do we design Phase 1 around: how many branches and companies, and is foreign currency needed on day one?
Permit to Issue. What does the standard application flow look like, and does one permit cover a group of branches?
QR code. What exact content will the final circular require, and who generates the code?
Numbering. Do we allow yearly series resets as a setting, or require one continuous series per branch and document type?
Invoice wording. Which validity or footer phrases still apply under current EOPT rules?
Core banking. Which core banking and source systems do we interface with first, and do we take summarised journals, full subsidiary ledgers, or both?
Trust books. Do we support trust and fiduciary books in the first pack, or leave them to a later phase?
Payroll. Which payroll systems do we support for journal and 2316 import first?
Technology. Which build stack do we choose, an open-source Python framework or DCP’s TypeScript stack, and who owns the decision?
Hosting and deployment. Cloud by default, and do we offer on-premise and a Philippine region for clients whose regulators expect it?
Filing partner. Which accredited electronic tax service provider do we work with for filing and payment?
Provider entity. Which DCP company in the Philippines signs as system provider and, if needed, as e-invoicing solution provider?
Point of sale. Do we need a POS partner for retail and food clients, or do we stay out of that market?
Commercial model. Subscription, licence plus support, or a build fee plus subscription, and do accounting firms get their own plan?
Name. Is LARA clear of trademarks in the software and services classes, is the domain available, and do we register the plain word or a lockup with Datum?
21. Glossary
AC: Acknowledgement Certificate, the BIR’s confirmation that a system is registered.
Annex B: the BIR checklist of functional and technical requirements for a CAS.
ATC: Alphanumeric Tax Code, used to classify withholding taxes.
CAS / CBA: Computerized Accounting System / Computerized Books of Accounts.
DAT file: the BIR’s text file format for alphalists and summary lists.
DST: Documentary Stamp Tax.
EIS: the BIR’s Electronic Invoicing System.
EOPT: the Ease of Paying Taxes Act and its regulations.
eTSP: Electronic Tax Service Provider, a company accredited by the BIR to file and pay taxes on behalf of taxpayers.
EWT: Expanded Withholding Tax.
FCDU: Foreign Currency Deposit Unit, the part of a bank whose foreign currency business is kept in separate books.
GRT: Gross Receipts Tax, the percentage tax paid by banks and similar institutions instead of VAT.
LARA: Ledger, Accounting, Reporting and Audit. DCP’s AI-native Computerized Accounting System described in this document, and the name of the assistant inside it.
PFRS: Philippine Financial Reporting Standards.
PTI: Permit to Issue, the BIR permit a taxpayer needs before issuing electronic invoices, separate from the Acknowledgement Certificate.
QAP: Quarterly Alphalist of Payees.
SAWT: Summary Alphalist of Withholding Taxes.
SLSP: Summary List of Sales and Purchases.
